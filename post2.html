Everyday tools that make life with low vision easier

September 10, 2026 · Practical Tips · Alex

When my vision changed, the first question I asked myself was: “How am I going to do all the ordinary things?” Over time I discovered a collection of tools and small adjustments that made daily life much more manageable. Here are the ones I recommend most often.

On your phone and computer

Modern devices already include powerful accessibility features. Turning them on is usually free and takes only a few minutes.

  • Screen magnification — zoom in on anything you need to see more clearly
  • Screen readers (VoiceOver on iPhone, TalkBack on Android, Narrator or NVDA on Windows) — they read text aloud
  • High-contrast modes and larger text — reduce eye strain immediately
  • Voice control — dictate messages, search, and open apps without looking

Around the house

Small physical changes often help more than expensive gadgets:

  • Brighter, even lighting in key areas (kitchen, bathroom, hallway)
  • High-contrast labels on appliances and switches
  • Tactile markers (small bumps or tape) on frequently used buttons
  • Organizing items in consistent places so you can find them by memory and touch

Helpful apps and devices

These have made a real difference for me and for many people I support:

  1. Magnifier apps that use the phone camera
  2. Color and light detection tools
  3. Simple voice assistants for reminders and information
  4. Portable video magnifiers for reading labels and menus when needed

One important reminder

No single tool works for everyone. What helps me might be different from what helps you. The goal is not to use every possible device — it is to find the combination that gives you back independence and confidence.

If you are just starting, begin with the built-in accessibility settings on your phone. They are powerful, free, and available right now.

I share these tips because they helped me, and I know how valuable practical information is when everything feels new. If you have tools that work well for you, I would love to hear about them.
